# ibara interface

The console is a fleet wall plus a list-and-focus view of one computer. The information hierarchy is fixed: the fleet, then one computer, then that computer's activity, files, access and system.

## Protected decisions

- Square shapes only. Every Rectangle has `radius: 0`; never use `Style.cornerRadius`. Status markers are small squares, except a person while a person has control and Omarchy's agent robot while agents work (Nerd Font glyphs, as Omarchy's own Agents button uses), so shape as well as color tells the states apart. Counts are plain text with thin separators, never pills or badges.
- **Nothing between the toolbar and the content (Tyler, 28 September; a hard rule).** Nothing ever sits between the fleet's toolbar and the computers, between a computer's header and its tabs, or between any page's header and its content, and nothing takes vertical space to show a message. Every message, approval, request, notification, attention item, error, note and result is a toast in the bottom-right corner (`Toasts.qml`), stacking upward and drawn over the page. A new message never moves the page. Confirmations before an action and Connect an Agent are cards attached to the control that opened them, drawn over the page too.
- Fleet first. The console opens on the fleet wall. Everything computer-specific lives inside a computer; there is no global Tasks, Files or System page.
- The quick panel is small and its main action is Open Console.
- Use the computer's directory label in primary text, never a raw computer id.
- Plain words. No jargon such as "root", "retained job", "safe file name" or "local handler response".

## Naming

ibara is always lowercase in user-facing text. This includes the bar tooltip, quick panel, console, window title, notifications, manifest display name and documentation. Keep the stable plugin ID `io.zet.ibara`. Code type and file names keep their existing casing.

## Visual language

Every color comes from the Omarchy theme; nothing is hard-coded, and a theme switch recolors every surface. Use Omarchy's font, scaling, surfaces and base colors. The bar mark uses the bar’s text color, including its wallpaper contrast on transparent bars. Marks in the console, focus and selection use `Color.accent`. Fleet states use the theme: attention `Color.urgent`, offline and locked `Color.muted`, and the theme palette's magenta (a person in control), yellow (paused) and green (ready), each falling back to `Color.accent` when a theme lacks it. A computer whose screen is locked reads Locked, "Screen locked · Take Control to unlock", counts under Needs Attention, and never blocks Take Control, which is how a person unlocks it. Agents working take the first of blue, cyan, bright cyan, bright blue, the accent and the text color that is clearly apart (CIEDE2000 12 or more, `Palette.distinct`) from magenta and from the red, yellow, green and muted of the other states: blue in most themes, cyan in Permafrost (whose blue is 8 from its magenta). In the all-gray White and Vantablack no color is, and the one that differs most is used; the person and robot marks tell those states apart. Pages from 1400 px wide use two columns (`Tokens.wideAt`), and a paragraph stays within about 90 characters (`Tokens.proseWidth`). Every state also has words; color alone proves nothing.

Every button is `ActionButton.qml`: one line of text sized to its label, in one of four roles and two sizes. Button text is Title Case ("Take Control", "Send File", "Open Viewer"): every word capitalized except a, an, the, and, or, to, of, in, on, for, with, by, from and at in the middle; ibara stays lowercase. Messages that name a button use its exact text. Primary fills with the accent and marks the one main action on a surface; a blocked primary loses its fill. Secondary is bordered and is the default. Danger uses the urgent color for its label and border. Quiet has no border at rest and serves back links, close, filters, crumbs and toggles in a bar. Size is normal (32 px, the height of a text field) or small (26 px, for card overlays, table rows and messages). A blocked button keeps its place and gives its reason in the tooltip; forms that need the reason visible show it as text under the button. Focus is always an accent border. Tabs and filters show the current choice with bold text and an accent underline, not a fill.

## Behavior

The quick panel and console share one service. Opening the console does not take control. Closing it never hands control back. Opening a computer scopes its administrator reads and actions to it; switching computer drops any pending confirmation and replaces the last computer's lists with the new one's last data, shown at once with its age (`DataAge.qml`) while its tabs read again. That kept data is memory only, per computer and identity, and goes on refusal, removal or denial. A confirmation names the computer, and nothing runs if the computer changed meanwhile.

Messages are toasts in the bottom-right corner (`Toasts.qml`, at most 560 px wide), drawn over the page under its header; they never move the page and nothing reserves room for them. The newest is at the bottom and older ones stack upward. Each toast is a square card on the popup background with a 3 px edge in its color: urgent for approvals and errors (with a faint wash), the palette's yellow for a request to use this computer, the accent for notes. Kinds and how long they stay:

- An approval (`ApprovalCard.qml`: "<computer> needs your approval", when it asked, the words, Approve, Deny, Always Allow on an agent's send, spend or delete, and Details; an agent's request to stop asking reads "<computer> needs your answer" with Allow and Not Now), an agent's question (`QuestionCard.qml`: "An agent on <computer> asks you", when it asked, the question, one button per offered answer or a field and Send, in Title Case on the button while the answer sent is the agent's own text, and Dismiss) and a request to use this computer (`PairRequestCard.qml`: who asks, the code, Accept and Decline) stay until answered here, on the notification or elsewhere. They show on every page, not only the fleet. Details opens labeled lines inside the toast (Who, What, Where, Page, Window title, Task), scrolling when long; the request itself is never shown, and Copy Request copies it exactly as the computer sent it.
- A login request (`LoginCard.qml`) is an approval in look and in how long it stays: "<computer> needs your approval", when it asked, the agent's request in words, a quiet ✓ toggle per site when there are several (only ticked sites go), Share (primary), Share With All Computers, Don't Share (danger) and Details (Who, Computer, Task, Sites, Page and a Note per site that signs in through another), where Never Share (danger) joins the buttons. Where it can't be answered it says so in place of the buttons: "Answer this on <computer>." (bold) on a console that doesn't share; "Login sharing is off." with Turn On Login Sharing (primary) and Don't Share; a signed-out site's sentence in the urgent color with Retry (primary) and Don't Share. The quick panel shows the same card, compact.
- An error stays until dismissed (✕ or Escape) or its condition clears; a dismissed refresh failure shows again only once it changes.
- A note leaves after 5 seconds, 10 with an action (Undo, Add Another, Show Fleet); a clean theme run after 5. What's New and the Connect an Agent pointer leave after 10 to 12 seconds; While you were away stays until it is read (Mark All Seen) or dismissed.
- Standing toasts last as long as their condition (`StatusModel.standingToasts`): the open computer's repair with Fix It and its files on their way (their progress along the toast's bottom edge), a site that rejected a shared login (urgent, with Take Control, until dismissed), What's New once after an update (dismissing it marks the notes seen), "Let your agents use your logins?" (`LoginSetupCard.qml`, accent: what ibara does, "Managed by your organization", the browsers and profiles found, Turn On, a browser and profile `ActionMenu` when there are several, and Not Now) until answered, Apply Theme to Fleet while it runs and its result with Apply Again and Details, While you were away with Details and Mark All Seen (dismissed, it shows again the next time the console opens), and, on the fleet until an agent has begun a task, one pointer to Connect an Agent (dismissed, it doesn't come back).

No toast leaves while the pointer or the keyboard is on it, while its details are open, or while it is hidden. The stack shows at most a few (5 in a 920 px window, fewer in a smaller one), but whatever waits for an answer (an approval, a login request, a question, a request to use this computer, "Let your agents use your logins?", ibara not running) always shows and never waits behind "+N more"; the newest notes fill the room left and older notes collapse first (`StatusModel.toastLayout`) behind a "+N more" toast whose Show All opens the whole list, scrolling when it is taller than the page, with Show Fewer to close it. Each message source (action error, action note, refresh failure, the page's own message) shows at most one toast, and a text already showing is not shown twice. Action messages clear on route change. The Omarchy desktop notification for an approval is separate and unchanged. Messages are plain English, and each names a real place or action in the console (a tab, a button, the fleet, Add computer) or says that ibara is handling it; nothing points to a page that does not exist. A transport failure names the computer that is not answering instead of showing ssh or tailscale output. Stale, denied, offline, paused and unsettled states stay distinct.

Preview cards stay mounted while frames update. The visible image changes only after the next frame has decoded, so a poll cannot flash an empty screen. Offline computers keep their last frame, grayed. Unauthorized or revoked computers clear it.

## Keyboard

- `/` focuses search.
- Arrow keys move across the grid or list; Left and Right switch tabs when the tab bar has focus.
- F6 moves the keyboard to the toasts (the first one in reading order: "+N more" when it shows, else the oldest shown) and back to the page. Tab moves through the toasts' buttons. In a toast, Escape closes its details, else dismisses it, else returns to the page; approvals and requests are never dismissed, only answered. When a toast with the keyboard goes, the keyboard moves to the toast in its place, else back to the page. Every toast button has a screen-reader name that says what it acts on ("Approve on Lumen: …").
- Enter opens. Escape cancels a confirmation, closes Connect an Agent, dismisses the newest message that can be dismissed, goes back to the fleet, then closes. It closes the console only from the fleet, never from inside a computer or Add computer. While renaming, Enter saves and Escape cancels only the rename.
- T, V and H act on the open computer, or on the fleet card with the keyboard: Take Control, Open Viewer or Close Viewer, and Hand Back. They do nothing while a text field has the keyboard or a confirmation is open. The viewer button follows the viewer the Take Control or Open Viewer answer names (`viewer_pid`), checked every 1.5 s by process name, so it turns back to Open Viewer when the person closes the viewer window; Close Viewer ends only the viewer, never the control.
- Tab follows reading order; in a computer it goes from the list to the tabs to the tab content.
- On an Access cell, Enter, Space or Down opens its list; Up and Down move; Enter chooses. Escape closes the list, or cancels the confirmation attached to a cell, and does nothing else.
- In a confirmation, Cancel has focus; Left, Right and Tab move between Cancel and the action, and Enter or Space chooses.
- F5 refreshes.

Fictional acceptance data belongs only in an isolated development launch, clearly labeled and unable to reach live targets. No demo fleet, fixture IPC or fixture setting ships.

`HandBackPills.qml` is owned by the service. Each held computer gets a compact Overlay layer pill at the top center of its viewer’s screen, stacked when several share a screen. It takes no keyboard focus and never moves or grabs the pointer. Hand Back uses the current expected owner/revision and closes the viewer. Open questions with no options or one affirmative answer offer Done & Hand Back through the question card’s answer-and-handover path; other questions offer Hand Back and Open. Ctrl+Alt+Shift+H hands back directly inside the viewer with or without keyboard grab; Super+Alt+Escape keeps its keyboard-toggle role. Closing a viewer alone keeps control.
